The two families

  • Rectilinear fields. Upright and horizontal planes of deliberately uneven width sit on open ground. There's no implied depth — the work is asymmetry held in balance, tuned by interval, weight, and a few quiet colors, after John McLaughlin.
  • Flat planes. A few large flat color planes divided by bold straight cuts — mostly steep diagonals, sometimes a clean divider — with strong value contrast and the occasional pair of same-hue values meeting at an edge to imply a folded plane.
  • Isometric. A field of stacked cubes shaded top-light, side-mid, side-dark under one light, filling the frame edge to edge — a close-up detail of a much larger stepped form. Depth is pure face value; nothing recedes but the color.
  • Emblem. A single mark on an open field, grown from rules rather than drawn from stored shapes: a field carved by random cuts repeated around a rotation group, a volume grown cell-by-cell on an isometric lattice, a self-avoiding walk drawn as one rounded stroke, or a generated polygon nested under shrink and rotation.
  • Shared discipline. Both keep to flat fills, a limited palette, and large shapes. Nothing is a gradient or a shadow — only color and angle.
