Video, off the rails

Five unconventional ways to wire a <video> element into a page, all built on your uploaded clip.

01 — Scroll-scrubbed

Scroll drives playback

Instead of autoplaying, the video's currentTime is bound directly to scroll position within this section. Scroll down slowly to "play" the clip.

02 — Hover-to-play grid

Only the hovered tile decodes

Three muted instances of the same video, each starting at a different offset. Playback only starts on hover — cheap on bandwidth and CPU when scaled to a real gallery.

03 — Ambient color extraction

The page reads the frame back

A hidden canvas samples pixels from the current video frame every few hundred milliseconds and averages them into a single color, which then drives the swatch and background tint in real time — press play.

#000000
04 — Text as a viewport

Footage plays through the letterforms

The video is masked so it's only visible where the headline text sits, using -webkit-background-clip: text. Everywhere else in the block, it's just a dark background.

05 — Split-screen compare

One clip, two treatments

Two synced instances of the same video — one styled, one not — revealed with a draggable clip line. Useful for before/after grading, color passes, or camera angle A/B.
