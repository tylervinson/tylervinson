Root cause analysis

Why the page count keeps climbing

Your index is not growing because you are publishing more. It is growing because a few dozen enormous PDFs get republished under new filenames, and DocAccess treats a new filename as a new document rather than a new version of an old one.

A provider directory runs 2,500 to 3,400 pages. Republish fifteen of them with a fresh date stamp in the filename and you have added 40,000 pages without writing a single new sentence. Nothing is removed when the old version is replaced, so every cycle is purely additive.

The mechanism

DocAccess identifies a document by its URL string. Per CivicPlus documentation, when a PDF changes at the same URL it is reprocessed in place. Change the URL and you get a second document instead, and the first one stays.

The tell: your filenames carry version stamps. 202510, 202601, 202610, py23, py24. Every stamp is a new URL, and every new URL is a new document with a full page count attached.

LEAK 05

Lower environments and dead links

mostly contained

Staging and development crawl the same shared Bynder and Sitecore URLs that production does, since neither of those systems has a lower environment. Cross-domain dedup absorbs 96,065 pages of that, so it is not inflating your total, but it is wasted crawl capacity. The two lower environments contribute only 180 documents and 2,408 pages that exist nowhere in production, which are most likely unpublished drafts that should not be member-facing at all.

Separately, 648 URLs return HTTP 404 and remain in the inventory. Dead links are never retired from the document list.

The structural fix

Cleanup buys you one reset. It does not stop the growth. The growth stops when directories are published to a stable URL.

Keep the date stamp in document metadata or in the link text if the business needs it visible. It just cannot be in the filename.

Recommended sequence

  1. Verify the 2027 twins. Open Document Settings on 2027_DSNP_PROVIDER_DIRECTORY_SPECIALIST_SPANISH.pdf and its 2026 counterpart. Compare MD5 fingerprints. This takes two minutes and validates the whole analysis.
  2. Check Auto-Enablement Preferences. Your Documents, button above the filters. Confirm whether "Documents Hosted on Other Domains" is on. Both your Bynder DAM and your Sitecore media library are external domains as far as DocAccess is concerned, which is 94% of your page volume.
  3. Disable plan year 2023 to 2025 directories. Filter by Hosted Site, sort by Pages descending, work down the list. Largest return per click available anywhere in the product.
  4. Add exclusion rules for staging.azblue.com and development.azblue.com in Domain Settings.
  5. Take the publishing convention to the web team. Stable URLs for directories. Without this, everything above repeats next quarter.
  6. Baseline the manifest. Pull the Document List URL from Domain Settings weekly and diff it. Thirty seconds of effort gives you a growth tripwire.
Methodology, assumptions and known limits
  • Source data. DocAccess inventory URL export dated 2026-11-03T20:10:53, 9,728 rows collapsed to 6,970 distinct URLs. Where one URL appeared under several domains the highest page count was kept. Supplemented by the inventory summary export of the same date and the Reports dashboard screenshot.
  • Page total discrepancy. This export yields 173,682 pages. Summary Statistics shows 167,131 and Document Age Analysis shows 170,896. The three figures almost certainly apply different inclusion rules for errored, unprocessed and excluded documents. All analysis here uses the export's distinct-URL figure for internal consistency.
  • Duplicate detection is filename-based. The URL export carries no MD5 column, so two genuinely different files sharing a name count as duplicates here, and the same file under two unrelated names does not. The 17,670 figure is approximate in both directions. MD5 fingerprints are available per document in the UI and should be used to confirm before disabling anything.
  • The 2027 twin finding rests on identical page counts, which is strong evidence but not proof of identical content. Confirm by fingerprint.
  • Plan year classification reads the leading year in the filename, falling back to a py## pattern and then to any four-digit year present. Files with a publication date stamp that differs from the plan year may be classified one year off.
  • Compliance caveat. Disabling superseded directories may conflict with ADA Title II retention obligations or CMS requirements for Medicare Advantage directory availability. Confirm with compliance before bulk-disabling. Disabling in DocAccess affects the accessible view, not the underlying PDF on your site.
  • Not established. Whether reprocessing counts toward billable volume, what your plan's page allowance actually is, and whether the Transcribe Content on Demand setting is contributing to the irregular jump cadence. The Transcription Activity panel shows individual formularies processed eight and nine times, which suggests reprocessing is tracked separately from the index total.
  • Growth figures. The 127,000 starting point is as reported verbally and was not verified against a prior export. A week-over-week export diff would convert the entire analysis from inference to measurement.
